0102	How to research a local government
0103	Finding one line of evidence in the city website and its long-term development plan
0104	🎯 01. Conquer the local government website ‘completely’ first
0105	Don’t finish your research with AI, news and portal searches. The core is **going deep into the chosen city or county’s own website first**
0106	🏛️ Public data
0107	📂 Information-disclosure material
0108	📊 Administrative and statistical material
0109	🗂️ Open-administration material
0110	→ **Securing primary sources the local government itself produces and maintains**
0111	Local government material contains not just information but **hints about what problems the region currently has and which direction it intends to move in**
0112	📚 02. The document you must find = the ‘long-term comprehensive development plan’
0113	Use the **long-term comprehensive development plan** that most local governments hold as your most important research source
0114	Example: **“Yeoncheon County 2030 Long-Term Comprehensive Development Plan”**
0115	🔍 What you can find in the document
0116	📌 Regional overview and current status
0117	📊 Analysis of regional conditions
0118	🧭 Long-term development vision
0119	🏙️ Sector-by-sector development plans
0120	⚙️ Implementation and management plans
0121	→ It runs to hundreds of pages, but **it is worth reading before you even choose your detailed topic or your local government**
0122	🧠 03. The long-term plan = the document that tells you what the local government wants
0123	Administrative material written by civil servants is not just paperwork; it is **material accumulated on the basis of a clear purpose and evidence**
0124	So reading the development plan lets you grasp
0125	🏛️ **“What kind of city does this local government want to become?”**
0126	which you can then work out
0127	In other words, **a process of grasping in advance the direction your client — the local government — wants**
0128	📊 04. One development plan connects ‘situation analysis → evidence → idea’
0129	The long-term development plan is not mere reference material; it can serve as **the evidence base for the whole proposal**
0130	**① 📊 Situation-analysis material**
0131	Regional status, population, local characteristics and so on
0132	→ **Use as the evidence for the proposal’s situation analysis**
0133	**② 🗣️ Resident opinion material**
0134	Resident surveys, interviews and so on
0135	→ **Use as qualitative evidence supporting the logic**
0136	→ Where needed, **usable much like FGI material**
0137	**③ 🧭 The values the city pursues**
0138	The goals and core values the local government has set for the city
0139	→ **A source for setting the direction of the idea**
0140	💡 05. Combining ‘what the local government wants’ with ‘our new perspective’
0141	The core is **not simply following the direction the local government set, but adding a perspective only students have**
0142	**The local government’s values**
0143	🌿 A peace city of coexistence and prosperity
0144	🏕️ An attractive city of ease and rest
0145	⬇️
0146	**Combined with a new student idea**
0147	→ Existing policy and direction + a new viewpoint
0148	→ **Developed into logic and ideas that are only ours**
0149	🎯 06. Why a ‘tailored proposal’ is persuasive
0150	Once you understand the local government’s direction
0151	**Grasp what the local government wants → set a proposal direction to its taste**
0152	**→ Add a new perspective to existing material → build our team’s own logic**
0153	**⇒ A proposal that stands apart**
0154	That is, not simply presenting a good idea but
0155	🎯 **“why our idea is needed by this particular local government”**

0174	Logic vs. Creative
0175	Redefining the problem, seen through the real Yangju and Pocheon cases
0176	🎯 Planning that connects logic and creative
0177	🧠 01. The essence of planning = a way to solve a problem
0178	Planning is not simply **producing a good idea**
0179	it is **the process of finding a problem and proposing a way to solve it**
0180	🔑 **A logical approach → finding the problem → the method (How) → creative**
0181	That is, creative is not a separate realm from logic; it is **the concrete method for solving the problem**
0182	🔗 02. Don’t think of logic and creative separately
0183	The dilemma people often fall into in competition planning
0184	📊 Focus only on logic → the idea risks being ordinary
0185	🎨 Focus only on creative → the idea lacks justification and persuasive power
0186	But it is not a choice between the two:
0187	**once you pin down a clear problem → the method for solving it becomes the creative**
0188	💡 **“Finding the way to solve the logic” matters more than “forcing an original idea”**
0189	🏙️ 03. Case ① Yangju｜Showing ‘all of Yangju’ rather than promoting attractions
0190	**The brief**
0191	→ Yangju, wanting to become a day-trip destination for the capital region
0192	At first we approached it as
0193	**listing the attractions → and promoting each one**
0194	But in the research we found **falling visitor numbers by living zone, and a gap in how the area was perceived**
0195	🔎 Redefining the problem
0196	Not simple attraction promotion, but
0197	**“the problem that people perceive Yangju as split into east and west”**
0198	was, we judged, the core problem
0199	💡 The creative
0200	**Developing a new tourist route that connects the living zones**
0201	A tourist path linking east ↔ west
0202	Connecting several living zones into one experience
0203	Leading people to perceive all of Yangju as a single destination
0204	→ **Grew into the message “Connecting Yangju”**
0205	📌 **The core:**
0206	**How you define the problem determines the direction of the creative**
0207	🌊 04. Case ② Pocheon｜Not ‘promotion’ but creating a reason to stay
0208	**The brief**
0209	→ Develop Pocheon, already known as a day-trip destination, into **a one- or two-night stay destination**
0210	At first we considered promoting the existing tourism business more aggressively
0211	But the research showed
0212	people still perceived Pocheon as **a day-trip destination**
0213	— that was the problem we found
0214	🔎 Redefining the problem
0215	Simply **“promoting it as a stay destination”** would not change perception
0216	→ **We needed a new tourist experience that actually makes people stay**
0217	💡 The creative
0218	Rather than changing the physical environment of the Hantan River
0219	**we combined it with ‘learncation’ (learning + vacation), a new tourism trend at the time**
0220	Drawing on the Hantan River’s geological and historical character
0221	→ Developed into **stay-type tourism content where learning and travel coexist**
0222	Realised as concrete tourism programmes, including ones for students
0223	📌 **The core:**
0224	**Interpreting existing resources through a new lens can lead to new creative**
0225	🎯 05. There is no ‘right formula’ in competitions
0226	CUIF winners are decided in directions that are hard to predict each year
0227	In some years
0228	→ **a logically solid team** is rated highly
0229	In other years
0230	→ **a team with overwhelming creative** is rated highly
0231	So there is no single right answer or standard winning formula
0232	What matters instead is
0233	**logic + creative + a persuasive pitch**
0234	holding all three together
0235	🏆 06. The structure of planning that gets close to the Grand Prize
0236	**① Logic**
0237	→ Find the local government’s problem clearly
0238	**② Problem definition**
0239	→ Draw out the core problem to be solved, not a mere symptom
0240	**③ Creative**
0241	→ Present a concrete method for solving the problem
0242	**④ Persuasion**
0243	→ Present why this idea is right for solving the problem
0244	⭐ **Good creative = not an original idea in itself, but an idea that solves a clearly defined problem**
0245	🔑 The core
0246	**❌ “How do we make a more original idea?”**
0247	**⭕ “How do we solve the problem we found?”**
0248	In the end,
0249	research → finding the problem → defining the problem → direction of the solution → creative → a persuasive pitch
0250	keeping that flow unbroken is the core
0251	💬 In one line
0252	**Logic gives birth to creative, and when creative solves the problem, a persuasive plan is complete**
0253	How to write the plan
0254	Designing a 1–3 month schedule counted backwards from pitch preparation
0255	⏱️ 01. The core of a plan = design the schedule around the speaker
0256	In a competition that runs 1–3 months, like CUIF, **systematic schedule management and a team process** matter
0257	The speaker is not there only to present; they have to understand the whole plan and the output well enough to deliver it in their own words
0258	So design the whole schedule on the premise of **securing at least 3–5 days for pitch preparation**
0259	🔑 Fit the schedule around the speaker, but do not simply follow whatever the speaker says
0260	🔎 02. Planning and research｜up to 7–10 days
0261	The stage running status analysis → finding the problem → drawing insight → setting the direction of the solution
0262	**Recommended: about one week, at most 10 days**
0263	⚠️ Avoid getting buried in one plan for too long
0264	If no direction emerges within a week, you need **the flexibility to drop it and switch to plan B**
0265	What to check at the end of the planning stage
0266	Is this a story only our team can follow?
0267	Is the logic graspable in one go at the pitch?
0268	Is it too complicated?
0269	Or conversely, is the logic too thin?
0270	💡 03. The idea｜up to 10 days
0271	The stage of **making the ideas needed for the situation analysis and the solution concrete, through a student’s own lens**
0272	A structure running insight → concept → execution plan
0273	**Make about 3–4 idea plans, compare and select**, then move to producing the material
0274	✨ You don’t have to find ideas only in the meeting room
0275	Actively collect the ideas that come to you while travelling or in everyday life
0276	🎨 04. Producing the material｜up to 14 days
0277	The stage of **completing the visual output of the proposal** — deck, images, video
0278	Allow **about two weeks of production**, allowing for collaboration and individual working time
0279	⚠️ Don’t obsess over the polish of the output
0280	The more time you spend the better it looks, but **eating into pitch preparation time hurts the overall result**
0281	🎯 What matters is not ‘the prettiest output’ but **the ability to visualise effectively within the time you have**
0282	🗣️ 05. Pitch preparation｜the flower of CUIF
0283	The stage of learning the plan well enough to **explain it fluently and convincingly**
0284	The speaker’s job is not to memorise a script but **to understand the whole material and explain it in their own words**
0285	If the speaker takes part in making the deck as well
0286	→ They understand why each page is laid out as it is
0287	→ They grasp the key message
0288	→ They know what to emphasise on each page
0289	→ They can present naturally without a script
0290	🏆 Pitching is **experience that carries beyond a winning strategy into communication and interview skills**
0291	🤝 06. The speaker is not ‘the person who only presents’
0292	The speaker is the core communicator, who must understand the material from A to Z
0293	So it matters that they **take an active part in making the deck and in the planning**
0294	Especially for anyone who wants to build presentation skills, the speaker role is itself good training
0295	🛍️ 07. If you are ambitious, ‘make it for real’
0296	If you can realise the idea as an actual output — merchandise, running a social account — **leave room in the schedule and go for it**
0297	If the university provides team activity funds or a subsidy, I recommend taking it all the way to production
0298	Real executed output is
0299	→ **a portfolio asset**
0300	→ **an added strength that raises your chance of winning**
